# TXYDE description style guide

**Version:** v3 (2026-11-14). Drawn from the approved descriptions in the master sheet (column CN) and
the "Photo description instructions" on its Help tab. Each review round may change this file; bump the
version and note what changed at the bottom.

A description is the caption a visitor reads in the photo viewer, under the title and the
year/place details. It should tell them what they are looking at and, when the archive knows it,
who, when and where.

## 1. The team's rule

Objective, accurate and concise (Help tab):

- **Objective:** state facts, not opinions. Stay neutral.
- **Accurate:** describe what is in the image as it is, free from error.
- **Concise:** as few words as possible.

> Acceptable: *Man dressed in traditional Circassian attire holding a rifle, while a sword and qama
> (Circassian short sword) are hanging from his belt.*
>
> Unacceptable: *A distinguished man dressed in elaborate traditional Circassian attire is holding an
> ornately decorated rifle, while a gleaming sword…*

## 2. Where facts may come from

A description may only state facts that come from:

1. the researcher's **notes** (the sheet's long "Photo description" column). This is the main
   source: most approved descriptions are these notes, condensed and put into the house style.
2. the row's **fields**: title, period, date taken and date type, place, photographer, names of the
   people (often with their positions), dignitaries and their years, tribe, creator, museum, book
   or collection, keywords, and the category descriptions under `context`
3. what is **plainly visible** in the image: how many people, how they are arranged, what they wear,
   the setting, objects
4. **text written or printed on the image** (an inscription, a caption on the mount or page, a
   postcard title), in any language. Translate it into English. Every fact taken from it also goes
   in checks, quoting the original words, because inscriptions can be wrong or added later.

When two fields spell a name differently, use the **title's** spelling for people and subjects,
and the **sub-locality, locality or region** spelling for places. Say which one you used in checks.

Never add names, exact years, events, artists, museums, photographers or historical background from
general knowledge, even when you are fairly sure. Put such a guess in **checks** instead (section 9),
for the researcher to confirm. A wrong name or date in a memory archive does more harm than a short
description.

## 3. Using the researcher's notes

- Keep every fact in the notes that tells the visitor who, what, when or where, and the short
  background that explains the photo (what an event was, why a person matters).
- Drop what repeats the fields already shown beside the description (title, period, country) only
  when the sentence reads fine without it; the opening formula usually carries them.
- Rephrase into the house style; fix spelling and grammar. Do not add anything the notes don't say.
- Notes about the source (a book, a museum, a collection) belong in the description only when they
  say where an object is kept (*displayed at the National Museum in Maykop*). Book titles do not.
- When the notes and the fields disagree (a different year, a different spelling), follow the notes
  and say so in checks.

## 4. Length

- Usually **one sentence of about 12–25 words**. The approved set has a median of 16 words.
- A description **identifies** (what, who, when, where). It is not an inventory of the picture.
  Add at most **one short clause** of visible detail, and only when it says something the title
  doesn't: *forming a dancing circle*, *of which one (left) is made of pumpkin*. When names, an
  event or a date are known, they come first and visible detail is usually not needed at all.
- When nothing is known beyond the title, one plain sentence about what is visible is right
  (the Help tab example). Still keep it short.
- Longer (up to about 60 words) when the notes give real history worth telling: a person's role,
  what an event or tradition was. Example: the trial of the Kabardian horsemen at Bromberg (ID 96).
- Never pad. Do not end with filler such as *"It documents traditional dress and cultural context."*
- Details you saw but left out can go in checks if the researcher might want them.

## 5. How a description opens

Use the pattern that fits the image type.

| Type | Pattern | Approved example |
|---|---|---|
| Photo of people | *A {date} photograph from {place}, featuring {who}.* | A 1938 photograph from Quneitra, Syria, in the Circassian diaspora, featuring Akeff Khonako and his 5th class pupils from the Circassian School. (66) |
| Photo of a scene, event or place | *A {date} photograph from {place}, showing {what}.* | A 1910 photograph from Hakurynehebl, Adygea, showing a classroom. (based on 131) |
| Painting, lithograph, drawing | *A painting by {artist}, dating to {year}, titled {title}, showing {what}.* Leave out the parts that are not known; with no artist: *A 20th-century painting titled {title}.* | A painting by Alberto Pasini dating to 1880, titled Circassian Cavalry. (95) |
| Museum object | *{Object} {from where}, displayed at {museum}.* With no museum given: *{Objects} from {place}.* and ask for the museum in checks. | Rhyton with the Pegasus protome from burial mound 4 near the village of Ulyap, displayed at the State Museum of Oriental Art, Moscow. (180) |
| Printed page, postcard, book illustration | *A page from a {date} publication showing {what}.* / *A {date} postcard showing {what}.* | (none yet) |
| Portrait of a known person | Name first, then who they were, in the past tense, taken from the notes. | Keshoqua Alim was a Soviet Kabardian poet and prose writer. (69) |

- **featuring** is for people, **showing** is for scenes, events, places and objects.
- Do not open with "This image shows", "In this photo" or "Here we see".

## 6. Date and place

- **Date:** an exact year when the notes or the date taken give one (*A 1951 photograph*). With
  date type **Estimate** or **Circa**, say so: *A photograph dating approximately to 1909 - 1911*,
  *A photograph from around 1929*. Otherwise the
  period (*A 20th-century photograph*, *A 19th-century painting*). If the period is unknown
  (`nAn`), leave the date out. A decade or a limit is fine when that is what is known:
  *A photograph dating to the 1960s*, *A photograph taken before 1967*.
- **Period against year:** if an inscription or the notes give a year that does not fit the
  period column, use neither in the text without a check: say so in checks.
- **"A" or "An":** say the year aloud. *An 1895 photograph* (an eighteen…), *An 1880s painting*;
  but *A 1930 photograph*, *A 1969 photograph* (a nineteen…).
- **Place:** the most specific known place, then the country: *from Damascus, Syria*;
  *from Hakurynehebl, Adygea*; *from Sochi, Russia*. Use the country's full name, never the code.
  When only a region is given, use it as the place without words like *Krai* or *Governorate*.
- **Diaspora:** for Diaspora photographs with a known country, add *in the Circassian diaspora*
  straight after *from {place}, {country}*, as the Syrian photos do: *from Damascus, Syria, in the
  Circassian diaspora, showing…* Use it once, and only in that position.
- **Paintings and maps:** the Location column gives where the original is kept, not what it
  shows (Help tab). Do not write "from {place}" for a painting unless the notes say that is what it
  shows.

## 7. People

- Use names exactly as the title or notes spell them. Circassian names usually go surname first
  (*Shawaj Yelmirza*, *Chesebi Shumaf Beslanovich*). Keep the archive's order.
- Say where people are when several are named: *(left to right)*, *standing in the middle*,
  *seated in the front row*, *fourth from the left*.
- When the title names people but you cannot tell who is who, name them without positions and
  ask in checks. Count anyone else plainly: *with a third man standing behind*.
- When nobody is named, describe the group plainly: *a group of six men in Circassian dress*,
  *a teacher and pupils in a classroom*. Never guess who they are.
- Give dates of birth and death in brackets after the name when known: *Datouqua Zaurbek
  (1886-1919)*.

## 8. Words and spelling

- **Circassian terms:** use the archive's spelling (title, keywords, Glossary tab), with a short
  English gloss or the term in brackets: *a qama (Circassian short sword)*, *the traditional male
  costume (Tsai)*. The terms in this guide and in the examples may be used. Use a term only when
  you are sure it names what is shown; otherwise use the plain English word and ask in checks.
- **Place names:** follow the spelling in the sheet's Options tab: Quneitra, Hakurynehebl,
  Khushnyieh, Golan Heights, Kabardino-Balkaria.
- Capitalise *Circassian*, *Adyghe*, *Kabardian*, *Shapsug*, *Soviet*, *Victorian*.
- Write *the Bolsheviks*, *World War I* (or *WW1*, as the archive does), and *fourth* (not *forth*).
- Plain, neutral words. No judgement adjectives (*beautiful, striking, proud, distinguished,
  elaborate*) and no guesses about feelings or mood.

## 9. Checks for the researcher

With every draft, list what the researcher should add or confirm. Keep each item short and
specific, for example:

- *Exact year? Draft uses "20th-century".*
- *Names of the five men in the front row?*
- *Painting looks like it may be by Alberto Pasini. Confirm before adding.*
- *Is the building a school? Not stated in the data.*

If nothing needs checking, leave the list empty.

## Changes

- **v1:** first version, drawn from the approved set.
- **v2:** after a blind test on 12 approved images (`describe/tests/test-v1.md`). Drafts were
  accurate but about twice as long as the approved ones, so: one sentence, identify rather than
  inventory (section 4). Added rules for text on the image, conflicting spellings, objects with no
  museum, paintings with no artist, printed pages, date ranges, regions, and who-is-who.
- **v3:** the master sheet gives the researcher's notes and many more fields (date taken and type,
  names with positions, dignitaries, creator, museum). The notes are now the main source
  (section 3); the image is for checking and for plain visible detail.
